He blotted it. He shut the book and put it back on the shelf, third from the end, where it stayed for twenty-six years and eleven months, until a woman with black hair and grey eyes cut the page out of it with a razor while the next Keeper stood in the doorway and did not stop her.

Then Ansel Bray went upstairs to bed and did not sleep, and never once, in the fifteen years remaining to him, told a living soul.

## EPILOGUE, The Road to Ashwater

She left Cairnmouth on the fourth day, riding north, because the vote had gone badly and she had said something at the stone table that she was going to have to answer for eventually.

The rain gave out around midday. The road went up through wet gold country with the sea somewhere behind and the smell of woodsmoke lying in the hollows, and she was thinking about nothing, which was itself unusual, and she passed a village whose name she did not catch.

Somebody was baking.

Seraine pulled the horse up so hard it swung.

She sat in the middle of the road in the middle of the afternoon with her heart going like a fist on a door and her eyes streaming, hunting through absolutely nothing for absolutely nothing, and after a while a boy came out of a gate to see if the lady was ill.

"No," she said. "No. Thank you. Something in the— no."

She rode on.

Two miles further, on a long empty rise with the whole ruined kingdom laid out below her, Seraine Voss took one hand off the reins and laid it flat and low across her own belly.

She did not know why she did it.

She could not have said, under oath, before the Chapter, at the point of a knife, why a woman who had been barren for twenty-two years and marvellous about it would put her hand *there*, of all places, on an empty road, and hold it, and hold it, and go on holding it while the light went long and gold across the hills.

Something under her palm turned over.

Very small. Very certain.

And four hundred miles south, in a war tent that smelled of horses and lamp oil, a king with no heir and a barren queen and a prophecy nailed to his conscience looked up from a map and said:

"Then send to Ashwater. Bring me the witch."
